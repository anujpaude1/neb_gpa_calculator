What it actually does

  • Exact answers by default. A fraction stays a fraction, and √8 comes back as 2√2 instead of 2.828. Press S⇔D when you want the decimal.

  • Complex numbers in both rectangular and polar form.

Questions people actually ask

Is it free?
Yes. No account, no trial period, no card. Every mode is open.
Does it really work like a Casio fx-991ES PLUS?
Closely. Same key layout, the same 403 functions, and SHIFT and ALPHA behave the way the yellow and red keys do on the handheld. The MODE menu has 8 entries: COMP, CMPLX, STAT, BASE-N, EQN, MATRIX, TABLE and VECTOR. One honest difference: the display writes on one line, so you get 19/24 rather than a stacked fraction.
Can I use it during the NEB board exam?
No. You cannot take a phone or laptop into the exam hall, so carry your physical calculator. Use this for homework, practice sets and checking answers.
Does it work offline?
Yes, after the first load. Install it as a PWA and it keeps working without internet, which matters when the wifi drops mid problem.
Can I use it on my phone?
Yes. The layout adjusts to small screens, and you can install it to your home screen like an app.
Does it do integration and differentiation?
Yes. Definite integrals, derivatives at a point, and summation, all from the keypad rather than a separate advanced mode.
Can it solve matrices?
Yes, up to 3×3. Determinant, inverse, transpose, multiplication, and vector dot and cross products.
Will it draw graphs?
No, and that is the honest limit. It computes, it does not plot. For graphs use Desmos or GeoGebra and keep this open for the actual arithmetic.
Is there a Chrome extension?
Yes. The WideMath Scientific Calculator runs the calculator in Chrome's side panel so you can keep your notes or a PDF on screen.
Does it save my history?
In your browser only. There is no account, so clearing your browser data clears the history and stored variables too.

Do I still need to buy a physical calculator?
For the board exam, yes. For everything else at home, this covers it.
